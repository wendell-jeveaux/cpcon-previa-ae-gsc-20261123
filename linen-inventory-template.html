Why a dedicated linen inventory matters

Linen is one of the largest re-usable assets a hotel, hospital or laundry owns — and one of the hardest to keep honest. Unlike normal stock, it is never in one place: at any moment a big share is in circulation on beds, on guests or on staff, another share is in wash, and only the rest is on the shelf. Count only the shelf and you will think you are short, over-order, and never see where the real loss is going.

A proper linen control spreadsheet follows the whole pool, not just the shelf — so you can right-size par levels, retire worn linen on a wash-cycle schedule, and finally attribute losses to a department. That is exactly what this template does.

What the template fixes

Linen disappears without a number

Towels and sheets walk out with guests, get binned by laundry or vanish between floor and linen room. Without a per-item count, the loss is invisible until the budget blows.

Stock-outs at the worst moment

Housekeeping runs out of king sheets at turndown, or the ward has no patient gowns at admission. Tracking in-stock against the par level flags the shortage before it happens.

Over-buying to feel safe

Teams over-order par stock to avoid running out, freezing cash in linen that sits in storage. A clear in-stock and in-wash split right-sizes the par level.

No idea what is in the laundry

A huge share of linen is in wash at any moment. With no in-wash column, that stock looks lost and gets re-bought needlessly.

Linen failing on the floor

Sheets and towels reach end of life and tear in front of a guest or patient. A wash-cycle count drives planned retirement instead of embarrassing surprises.

Loss never gets attributed

Without a department column, shrinkage cannot be traced to a floor, a shift or a laundry. The losses figure stays a mystery no one owns.

How to use the linen inventory template

Five steps take you from a vague feeling that linen is going missing to a controlled, reorder-on-time operation with a real loss figure.

  1. 1

    List your items and departments

    Enter each linen, towel, uniform and textile, and the department it is issued to. Do it once and keep it as your master list.

  2. 2

    Set the total (par) quantity

    Record the full stock you own for each item. This par level is what every other number is measured against.

  3. 3

    Split the stock by state

    Fill in how much is in circulation, in wash and in stock. The three should reconcile to the total — gaps point straight to losses.

  4. 4

    Log losses and wash cycles

    Each period, record items written off and update the wash-cycle count. This is where the real cost of linen finally becomes visible.

  5. 5

    Act on the status flag

    Use the OK / REORDER status to reorder on time and to spot the departments and items bleeding the most stock.

What does the linen inventory template track?+

It tracks each item by department with total (par) quantity, how much is in circulation, in wash and in stock, plus losses, wash cycles and an automatic OK / REORDER status. It works for hotel and hospital linen, laundry operations and staff uniforms.

Does it work in Excel and Google Sheets?+

Yes. The template is delivered as a CSV file, so it imports directly into Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice Calc. The columns are already structured for linen, laundry and uniform control; just import and start entering your stock.

Can I use it for hotel linen, hospital linen and uniforms?+

Yes. The Department column lets you separate housekeeping, F&B, spa, wards and operating theatres, and the item rows cover towels, bed linen, table linen, gowns, scrubs and uniforms — so a hotel, a hospital and an industrial laundry can all use the same model.

How is a linen inventory template different from a normal inventory sheet?+

Linen never sits still. At any moment a large part of your stock is in circulation or in the wash, not on the shelf. A generic inventory sheet only shows what is in stock; this linen template splits stock across in circulation, in wash and in stock, and adds wash cycles, so you see the whole pool, not just the shelf.
